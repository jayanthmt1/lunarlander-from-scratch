What you are watching

Eight numbers describe the lander: where it is, how fast it is going, which way it is tilted, and whether each leg is touching. Four things it can do: fire the main engine, fire either side thruster, or nothing. Every weight starts random, and the only feedback is the score.

The first few hundred attempts all crash. Then it learns to slow its descent — and stops there. Landing is worth about +260; hovering until the 1,000-step clock runs out is worth around +140; crashing on a landing attempt is worth about −40. Hovering beats crashing, and the gap between hovering and landing is a few precisely-timed steps that random exploration almost never finds. So it hovers, forever.

The pad sits in the middle by default, as it does in stock LunarLander. You can move it, or have it jump somewhere new every episode. The network never learns where the pad is — the first input is its distance from the pad, so a policy trained on a centred pad follows a moved one immediately. Slide the pad while it is hovering and watch it chase.

That plateau is the default here because it is the honest result. Open Parameters and raise the hidden layer to 32 — landing goes from 0% to 87%. A time penalty of 0.20 does it too. The algorithm was never the problem; 212 weights sits just under the capacity it needs.
